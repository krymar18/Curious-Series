BD Lab

Research a Client. Build the Pitch. Pressure-test It.

Jump to the lab Review AI Overview first
Objective

Research a real account, build its persona, and draft a proposal that focuses on specific priorities rather than a generic pitch.

Key idea

Break a complex prompt into smaller sections that build on each other's results, for tighter control over each factor.

BD Lab: Client Research to Proposal ~60 min

Seven steps, one account. Pick a real prospect or client before you start — this only works with a name you can actually search for.

Before you start

Have a company in mind, the Ampersand media kit ready as a PDF, and one Copilot Chat window you won't close until Step 7. Type the client's name below once — every prompt on this page fills in [COMPANY NAME] automatically as you type.

1
Company & Market Research
Copilot Chat + Web ~10 min

Use Copilot's web search to pull a broad picture of your client before you build anything. Pick a research focus below, then paste the prompt it builds. Thin public information is fine — Copilot will flag what it can't verify.

  • Open Copilot Chat (copilot.microsoft.com or via M365)
  • Confirm the web search toggle is on
  • Pick a focus, then paste the generated prompt below
Good sources for this research
What Copilot's web search should actually be drawing from
Company-owned

Official site, press releases, investor relations, product pages — their own framing of themselves.

Financial & regulatory

Earnings calls and annual reports for public companies; funding announcements for private ones.

Trade & business press

Ad Age, Marketing Dive, Digiday, regional business journals — how the industry covers them.

Leadership signals

LinkedIn posts and bios, interviews, conference talks — what leadership is prioritizing out loud.

Hiring signals

Open job postings often reveal budget shifts before press does — a "Head of Brand" req signals a brand push.

Competitive & analyst coverage

How competitors and analysts describe this company's position — feeds the Competitive angle focus below.

Research focus
Prompt — Company & market research

You are a B2B media research analyst preparing a client briefing. Research [COMPANY NAME]. Think step by step before you answer. 1. Company overview: what they sell, who their target customer is, and what markets or geographies they operate in. 2. Business priorities and pressures: current challenges related to customer acquisition, brand awareness, competitive pressure, or marketing strategy. Use recent news or earnings coverage if available. 3. Advertising posture: how they currently approach TV and video advertising, what we know about their media mix, agency relationships, or recent campaign activity. Summarize each section in 2-3 paragraphs. Flag anything that is uncertain or hard to verify. Do not invent figures — cite what you found or mark it as an estimate.

Prompt digest

Role, context, task, and format — broken down.

Role

"B2B media research analyst" narrows Copilot's vocabulary and judgment toward what a media seller actually needs, not a generic company summary.

Context

[COMPANY NAME] plus the web search toggle — Copilot is told to look, not guess from training data.

Task

Three numbered sub-asks, in order. The focus buttons above swap what these ask for, but the shape stays the same.

Format

2-3 paragraphs per section, uncertainty flagged explicitly instead of smoothed over.

Strategies used

Carry forward: copy the full response into a blank Word document before moving to Step 2.

Lifehack

Happy with how this prompt turned out? Hover over it in Copilot Chat, click the bookmark icon, and choose Save prompt — give it a name like "Client Research – Ampersand." It'll sit under View Prompts → Your prompts, ready to reuse on the next account without retyping anything.

2
Define Priorities & Build Client Persona
Copilot Chat ~10 min

Take the raw research from Step 1 and turn it into a structured, reusable client persona. Paste your Step 1 output into the same Copilot session before running this prompt.

  • Paste your Step 1 research directly into Copilot Chat
  • Then run the prompt below
Prompt — Client persona

Acting as a BD strategist writing this for your own use before a client meeting, use the research above to do two things. Work through each step before producing the final output. 1. Extract the top 3 business priorities for [COMPANY NAME] most relevant to a media investment conversation. For each one, name the specific piece of evidence from the research that supports it, then explain why it matters to a media partner. 2. Build a structured client persona with these sections: - Company snapshot (2-3 sentences) - Key decision-maker priorities (3-5 bullets, each tied to evidence) - What they care about in a media partner - Likely objections to a new proposal - How they measure success - Communication and relationship style Write the persona in third person, as a clean standalone document with clear section headers, ready to save and bring into a client meeting. Do not include a priority you can't tie back to something in the research above.

Prompt digest

Role, context, task, and format — broken down.

Role

"BD strategist writing for your own use" keeps the tone internal and candid, not client-facing polish yet.

Context

"The research above" — deliberately contextual. This prompt does nothing on its own without Step 1 still in the thread.

Task

Extract priorities with evidence, then build the persona from those same priorities — the second can't drift from the first.

Format

Third person, six named sections, standalone document.

Strategies used

Note

Save the persona into its own file — name it [ClientName]_Persona.docx — before moving on. You'll need it again at Steps 4 and 7.

Tool Spotlight: Make the Persona Reusable With Copilot Notebooks

Copilot Notebooks (Microsoft 365 Copilot and Copilot Chat) is a grounded workspace: you add reference sources — files, chats, pages — and Copilot answers only from what you've added. It's a better home for this persona than a chat thread that eventually scrolls out of reach.

  • The first time you run this lab, create a notebook — something like "Client Personas."
  • Add your Step 1 research output and Step 2 persona document to it as references.
  • Next time you touch this account, open the notebook instead of a blank chat. Ask it directly for updates or a refreshed angle — it stays grounded in what you already gathered.
  • Add each new client's persona to the same notebook as you build them, so nothing important lives only in a chat thread that ages out.

Lifehack

Notebooks stay in sync with their references — if you update the persona doc later, the notebook's answers update with it. No need to re-paste anything.

More detail: Get started with Microsoft 365 Copilot Notebooks (support.microsoft.com).

3
Email Debrief via Outlook Copilot
Outlook Copilot ~8 min

Use Copilot inside Outlook to summarize your email history with this client and surface what matters for your next conversation.

  • Open Outlook and find the Copilot button in the ribbon or sidebar
  • Replace [COMPANY NAME] in the prompt below
Prompt — Email relationship debrief

Acting as my executive assistant reviewing our email history, look at my recent communications with [COMPANY NAME] and give me three things. Before each section, note your confidence — high, medium, or low — based on how much email history actually exists. 1. Topic summary: what subjects have come up, what commitments were made, and where the conversation currently stands. 2. Relationship read: how would you characterize the current state of this relationship? Is there momentum, any unresolved issues, or open follow-up items? 3. Next contact prep: the 2-3 most important things I should reference or address in my next outreach, based on this history. If the history is thin, say so directly rather than filling the gaps with assumptions.

Prompt digest

Role, context, task, and format — broken down.

Role

"Executive assistant reviewing email history" — a role built around thoroughness and discretion, not opinion.

Context

Grounded in Outlook's own data, not general knowledge — Copilot can only summarize what's actually in the mailbox.

Task

Summarize, read the relationship, prep next contact — ordered so the third builds on the first two.

Format

A confidence rating per section, so a thin mailbox doesn't read as a confident answer.

Strategies used

Note

Little email history with this client? That's fine — thin results are still useful signal. The first time through, try a client you email often, just to see the feature working end to end.

4
Synthesize Research + Relationship Context
Copilot Chat ~7 min

Combine the external research with your email relationship context into a single brief. Paste both the persona document (Step 2) and the Outlook summary (Step 3) into a new Copilot Chat session.

  • Open a new Copilot Chat session
  • Paste the persona document and the Outlook email summary
  • Then run the prompt below
Prompt — Unified client brief

You now have two sources on [COMPANY NAME]: an external research persona and an internal email relationship summary. Acting as a BD strategist prepping for a proposal conversation, do three things in order: 1. Combine both sources into a single client brief. Where the public research and the internal relationship history disagree or create tension, flag it explicitly rather than picking one. 2. Identify the strongest opening angle for an Ampersand pitch right now: the specific problem we're best positioned to solve, and why now. 3. Before finalizing, check your own answer: what's the weakest link between the evidence and the opening angle you chose? State it in one sentence.

Prompt digest

Role, context, task, and format — broken down.

Role

Same BD strategist role as Step 2 — kept consistent on purpose, since it's the same work continuing.

Context

Two prior outputs, named explicitly as "external" vs. "internal" so Copilot doesn't blend them silently.

Task

Combine, then decide, then self-check — three ordered moves rather than one open-ended ask.

Format

Deliberately open — a brief, an angle, and one sentence. No template forcing it.

Strategies used
5
Develop Proposal with Media Kit
Copilot Chat + PDF Upload ~10 min

Upload the Ampersand media kit as context and use it alongside your client brief to build a proposal framework tied to this client's specific priorities.

  • In Copilot Chat, use the file upload button (paperclip icon) to attach the media kit PDF
  • Paste your unified brief from Step 4 into the same session
  • Then run the prompt below
Prompt — Proposal framework

Acting as a senior Ampersand account strategist, use the uploaded media kit and the client brief on [COMPANY NAME] to do two things. 1. Identify the top 3 Ampersand capabilities that are the strongest fit for this client's stated priorities. For each one, name the specific line in the client brief it answers — not a generic industry benefit. 2. Draft a proposal narrative structured as: - The challenge we are solving for them - Why Ampersand is the right partner - The specific capabilities we are recommending and why - What success looks like for them Keep the narrative tight — this is a framework, not a final document. Avoid restating capabilities the client brief doesn't call for.

Prompt digest

Role, context, task, and format — broken down.

Role

"Senior Ampersand account strategist" — client-facing polish starts here, one notch more formal than Steps 2 and 4.

Context

The uploaded PDF plus the Step 4 brief — two grounded sources instead of general industry knowledge.

Task

Match capabilities to specific brief lines first, then draft — so the narrative has to trace back to something real.

Format

A fixed four-part narrative shape, reused across every account so proposals stay comparable.

Strategies used

Note

If PDF upload isn't cooperating, paste 3-4 key capability descriptions from the media kit manually instead. The prompting pattern is what matters, not the upload button.

6
Brainstorm New Offerings
Copilot Chat ~8 min

Push beyond the standard pitch. Pick an angle below, then use Copilot to generate ideas along that line before pressure-testing the strongest one.

  • Continue in the same session with the media kit and client brief still loaded
  • Pick a brainstorm angle, then run the generated prompt
Brainstorm angle
Prompt — Brainstorm and pressure test

Set aside the standard pitch for a moment. Think like a BD strategist brainstorming out loud: bold first, practical second. 1. Given what you know about [COMPANY NAME]'s business goals and Ampersand's capabilities, generate 4-5 creative or non-obvious ways we could create value for them. Mix new use cases, bundled offerings, partnership angles, and data-driven approaches. Be specific to this client, not generic. 2. Of those ideas, identify the one with the highest potential and lowest barrier to execution. Explain why in 2-3 sentences. 3. Reframe that idea as a single compelling pitch point — 2-3 sentences in plain business language — that I could add to my proposal.

Prompt digest

Role, context, task, and format — broken down.

Role

"BD strategist brainstorming out loud" explicitly permits ideas that aren't fully baked yet.

Context

Same media kit and client brief already loaded — no new information, just a different lens on it.

Task

Generate wide, then narrow, then compress to one pitch — three different task types back to back.

Format

4-5 ideas, one winner with a reason, one plain-language pitch point.

Strategies used
7
Evaluate Through the Persona Lens
Copilot Chat ~7 min

Run your proposal and new pitch point back through the client persona. Ask Copilot to stress-test the pitch from the client's perspective before it reaches a real conversation.

  • Paste the persona document from Step 2 into the session
  • Reference your proposal framework and brainstorm pitch point from Steps 5-6
  • Run the prompt below
Prompt — Persona evaluation and refinement

I'm going to share a client persona and a proposal. Fully adopt the decision-maker's perspective from the persona — not a generic reviewer's — and do three things in order. 1. In character as that decision-maker, react honestly to the proposal: what lands, what doesn't, and what questions or objections come up in a first meeting. 2. Still in character: name the single weakest part of this proposal from your seat, and what would need to change for you to take it seriously. 3. Step out of character. Give me 2-3 specific edits that address those objections. Do not soften the objections in step 3 just because you're now the one being critiqued.

Prompt digest

Role, context, task, and format — broken down.

Role

Full persona adoption, not just a role label — Copilot argues from inside the character, not a description of how they'd feel.

Context

The persona doc plus the proposal and pitch point from Steps 2, 5, and 6 — this only works if all three are still in view.

Task

React in character, name the weakest point in character, then step out to fix it — the switch is deliberate and explicit.

Format

Three parts, ordered so the objections are on the record before the fix is offered.

Strategies used

Note

This is the highest-value step in the lab, and the one people skip most. Sit with the objections in parts 1 and 2 before you read part 3 — the discomfort is the point.

Lifehack

If a version of this prompt reliably catches good objections, save it and share it to your team through Prompt Gallery (Your prompts → Share prompt → Share to team) — so every rep runs the same pressure test before a proposal goes out, not just the reps who happen to know this trick.

Wrap-up

What You'll Walk Away With

  • A client persona document ready for a real meeting
  • A unified brief with your strongest opening angle
  • A proposal narrative tied to specific priorities, not generic positioning
  • One sharp, plain-language pitch point, already pressure-tested from the client's side
  • 2-3 concrete edits that address the objections you'd actually get

Conclusion & Reflections

A few minutes here is what turns a one-off exercise into a habit you'll actually reuse.

Key Takeaways
  • The chain only works if you stay in one thread. Paste forward, don't start over.
  • A persona is only as useful as its specificity. Vague roles produce vague simulations.
  • Evaluation prompts need explicit criteria. "Review this" gets you nothing you can act on.
  • The proposal that survives Step 7's objections is the one worth bringing to the real meeting.
Reflection
  • Which step produced the biggest gap between what you expected and what Copilot gave you?
  • Did the persona in Step 7 raise an objection you hadn't considered? What would you change because of it?
  • Where did you catch yourself wanting to skip a step? What did skipping it save you, and what did it cost?
