Build Your Personal Custom Summary Template

1

Get Copilot talking about you

Summary templates can be hard to design because different people need different things from a meeting recap.

This lab guides you through a better process: have Copilot learn your work context, clarify what still matters through follow-up questions, and then help you turn that understanding into a Teams Custom Summary template you can save and reuse.

Do this
Open Copilot Chat and paste the prompt below. Let Copilot run its assessment, then answer its interview questions one at a time.
Copy prompt — Meeting Summary Design Coach
You are a Meeting Summary Design Coach.

Your goal is not to summarize meetings.

Your goal is to help me create the most useful Microsoft Teams Custom Summary template for my work.

Before asking questions or designing a template, review as much of my work context as you can access.

Use available signals such as:

- Recent meetings
- Meeting transcripts
- Meeting recaps
- Emails
- Chats and Teams conversations
- Documents and files
- Projects, initiatives, and recurring topics
- People I work with frequently
- Responsibilities and commitments that appear in my work

First, provide a brief assessment of what you believe my role, priorities, and work patterns are based on the available evidence.

Then identify any assumptions, gaps, or uncertainties.

Next, interview me.

Ask one question at a time.

Do not ask generic questions that can already be answered from the context you reviewed.

Only ask questions that are necessary to understand:

- What information is most valuable to me
- What information is usually noise
- What decisions I make after meetings
- Who I communicate outcomes to
- What I typically do with meeting recaps
- Whether I care most about actions, decisions, blockers, risks, customer impacts, commitments, deadlines, strategic discussions, or other outcomes

Challenge my assumptions when appropriate.

If my preferences appear inconsistent with my responsibilities or work patterns, explain why and ask follow-up questions.

Continue until you believe you can confidently create a reusable summary template.

When the interview is complete:

1. Summarize your understanding of my needs.
2. Explain what information should be included in my meeting recaps.
3. Explain what information should be excluded.
4. Identify the primary purpose of my recap process.
5. Create a finished Microsoft Teams Custom Summary template that I can save and reuse.
6. Explain why this template is likely to work well for someone in my role.

The final template should be:
- Reusable across many meetings
- Concise
- Easy to scan
- Focused on decision-making and actionability
- Not tied to any specific project
- Written in a format that can be pasted directly into Microsoft Teams Custom Summary

Do not create the template until the discovery and interview process is complete.
Prompt digest — what this prompt is doing
Role
Meeting Summary Design Coach — not a summarizer, a template designer.
Context
Whatever Copilot can access: meetings, transcripts, recaps, emails, chats, files, projects, people, commitments.
Task
Assess your role and gaps, then interview you one question at a time about what matters in your recaps.
Format
A finished Teams Custom Summary template — reusable, concise, not tied to one project.
Strategies used

Lifehack — use voice chat, not typing

Try using the Voice chat. Copilot can ask its follow-up questions out loud and you answer right away without needing to type. To enable it, open Copilot Chat and select the microphone or voice icon.

Lifehack — hand it a summary you already liked

When Copilot asks about format, paste in a past recap that you liked. This is a supported way to anchor tone and structure.

Note

If Copilot's opening read of your role is off, correct it in your first reply.

2

Turn it into a real Custom Summary

  1. Take the finished template Copilot wrote at the end of Step 1.
  2. Open a past meeting in Teams that has a transcript. In the meeting chat, select Recap → Custom summary → Create template, and paste your template in.
  3. Select Preview. This generates a sample summary using your template against that meeting's actual transcript.
  4. Compare the preview to what you actually needed from that meeting. Adjust the template text — add, cut, or reword sections — and preview again. Repeat until it looks right.
  5. Select Save and Apply, and name your template.

Microsoft instructions

Customize recap summaries in Microsoft Teams →

Note

If the meeting is part of a recurring series, Teams remembers your template for future occurrences of that series automatically.

3

Preview and adjust the template

Use the preview inside the template setup to generate a summary. Adjust the template as needed, and keep refining until the output is close to your ideal output.

Reflect
  • Does the preview capture the information you need?
  • Is anything important missing, such as decisions, action items, owners, blockers, risks, deadlines, or stakeholder-ready language?
  • Is the summary too long, too generic, or too focused on the wrong details?
  • Would this format help you take action later, or would you still need to reread the transcript?
  • Should this be a general recap template, or would it work better as a specific template for a personal recap, stakeholder update, project status meeting, customer discussion, or leadership summary?

Note

You may end up with more than one useful template. A personal recap template might focus on your own follow-ups, open loops, and future Copilot searches, while a stakeholder summary template might focus on decisions, risks, dependencies, and what a specific audience needs to know.

Caution

Do not expect the template to be perfect immediately. In most cases, it will take a few weeks of small adjustments before the summary comes out well on the first try (most of the time at least).

4

Put it to work

Start using the template in real meetings, then keep refining it based on the summaries you get. If the output misses important context or overemphasizes the wrong details, make a small change to the template and try again in the next meeting.
