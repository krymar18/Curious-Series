Copilot Chat

A practical reference for the command centre of M365 Copilot — the only surface that simultaneously queries your email, Teams, SharePoint, calendar, and the web from a single conversation.

Objective

Use Copilot Chat to synthesise across your full M365 environment, find files by topic rather than filename, turn AI output into shared living documents, and run research that combines your internal data with current public information.

Key idea

Copilot Chat sees more of your work context than any individual tool. Cross-app synthesis is its highest-value capability and its most error-prone. Any synthesis you act on or share should be verified against source content for its most consequential claims.

Everyone in this training has M365 Copilot. Expand for the full tier comparison — the free tier is available to all M365 users but only works on public web content.

Work mode vs Web mode

Copilot Chat operates in two grounding modes. Work mode draws on your M365 tenant data (emails, files, chats, meetings) and stays within your M365 security boundary. Web mode draws on Bing-indexed public content. In a work context, default to Work mode unless you specifically need current public information.

Features

Cross-app synthesis, file search, Copilot Pages, web research, and agents — all from a single conversational interface.

Cross-App Synthesis — The Core Capability

The highest-value use of Copilot Chat: synthesising context across email, Teams, SharePoint, calendar, and meeting recaps to answer a question no single app could answer alone.

Cross-Source Queries & Work Catch-Up M365 Copilot
Excellent at
  • Project briefings that pull from emails, Teams chats, SharePoint, and meeting recaps simultaneously
  • Work catch-ups after absence — surfacing what happened across all channels while you were away
  • Pre-meeting context synthesis: what you last discussed, outstanding commitments, likely agenda items
Better avoided for
  • Informal content — quick Teams messages, one-line emails — is often missed by synthesis
  • Open discussions presented as reached conclusions — always verify consequential claims against source content
  • Coverage gaps after absence: Copilot may miss content from channels it didn’t index; supplement with manual activity review
Project briefing

Give me a one-paragraph briefing on [project/topic]. What has been decided, what is still open, what do I need to action? Sources: my emails and Teams from the last two weeks. Cite your sources.

Work catch-up after absence

I was out [time period]. What did I miss? Cover: emails needing a reply, key decisions made in Teams, and any meetings that happened without me.

Exercise — Cross-App Synthesis in Practice
  1. Choose a project that has had activity in email, Teams, and at least one meeting in the last two weeks.
  2. Use the project briefing prompt above. Review the output against what you know to be true.
  3. Identify: one thing it got right, one thing it missed, and one thing it misframed or oversimplified.
  4. Reflection: What would you need to change about how your team communicates for this to be reliably accurate?
File Search & Context IQ M365 Copilot
Excellent at
  • Finding files by describing what they contain rather than what they’re named
  • Scoping a query to a specific channel, file, or person using the ‘/’ key (Context IQ)
  • Comparing two documents side by side — contracts, proposals, policy versions — without opening either app
Better avoided for
  • Poorly named files in deeply nested folders — findability degrades with bad information architecture
  • Files you don’t have permission to see — Copilot Chat works within permissions, not around them
  • Assuming results are exhaustive — files that aren’t indexed or accessible won’t surface
Topic-based file search

Find all documents in my SharePoint and OneDrive related to [topic] from the last [timeframe]. Return the five most relevant with a one-sentence description of each.

Document comparison

Based on /[file 1] and /[file 2], what are the key differences in how these two [proposals / contracts / approaches] handle [specific issue]?

Copilot Pages, Web Research & Agents

Copilot Pages turns AI output into a shared living document. Web mode brings current external information into the conversation. Agents extend the capability further.

Copilot Pages M365 Copilot
Excellent at
  • Turning a Copilot output into a shareable, collaborative Loop document that the team can edit and contribute to
  • Creating a running project status page that replaces status email chains
  • Building a shared research document without leaving M365
Better avoided for
  • Pages require Loop to be enabled in your tenant — verify with IT before building workflows around it
  • Treating a Pages document as authoritative without the team verifying and contributing to it
  • Confusing Pages with SharePoint pages — they are Loop documents (informal, fast, collaborative), not published intranet content
Turn output into a Page

Turn this output into a Copilot Page I can share with [team]. Add a section for open questions they can contribute to.

Web Research & Model Selection M365 Copilot
Excellent at
  • Current industry news, competitor context, regulatory updates, and benchmarking data not in your internal files
  • Background research for proposals requiring up-to-date public information
  • Selecting Claude for long document analysis or nuanced writing tasks; GPT-5.5 for structured data and analytical reasoning
Better avoided for
  • Treating web-sourced claims as verified — Bing-indexed content varies widely in source quality and recency
  • Using web mode when work mode would answer the question from your internal data
  • Switching models mid-conversation — start a new conversation when changing models to avoid context inconsistencies
Web research briefing

Using web search, brief me on [topic]. What is the current state, who are the key players, and what has changed in the last 12 months? Cite sources and note anything particularly recent.

Ampersand Spotlight — Using This Responsibly

Copilot Chat can see more of your work than any individual tool. That cross-channel visibility is its core value — and its most significant tension.

Cross-app visibility dissolves channel boundaries

Email, Teams, and meetings exist as separate channels partly for reasons — some things are discussed there for speed, some for audit trail, some because participants wanted a private conversation. A cross-app query surfaces content from all channels simultaneously, regardless of the intent behind the original communication. Be deliberate about what you ask it to synthesise.

Synthesis errors compound

When Copilot combines information from multiple sources, it may conflate projects sharing a name, present an open discussion as a conclusion, or miss a critical Teams message that would change the summary. Any cross-app synthesis used for a decision should be verified against source content for its most consequential claims.

Web sources are not all equal

When Copilot uses web grounding, it cites sources — but not all sources are equal. The formatting of Copilot’s response does not distinguish between a peer-reviewed analysis and vendor marketing copy. Source quality evaluation is your responsibility, not Copilot’s.

Agents may be metered

Some agents in Copilot Chat are pay-per-query rather than covered by your flat licence. This applies to certain third-party agents and compute-intensive Microsoft capabilities. Check with IT which agents have metered pricing before including them in team workflows.

Conclusion & Reflections

Take a moment to reflect — it’s what makes the learning stick.

Key Takeaways
  • Copilot Chat is the only M365 surface that queries email, Teams, SharePoint, calendar, and the web simultaneously — that cross-signal capability is its core value.
  • Cross-app synthesis is the most powerful feature and the most error-prone. Verify consequential claims against source content before acting or sharing.
  • Use Context IQ (the / key) to scope queries to specific channels, files, or people when you want precision over breadth.
  • Copilot Pages turns AI output into a collaborative living document — use it to replace status email chains with a shared, editable record.
  • Work mode stays in your M365 boundary; Web mode uses Bing. Default to Work mode unless you need current public information.
Reflection
  • Copilot Chat can brief you on a project by reading your emails, Teams chats, and meeting notes simultaneously. What does that change about how you manage information across those channels?
  • A cross-app synthesis gives you a confident-sounding summary. What would you verify before acting on it?
  • If Copilot Chat can surface everything a colleague has said about a topic across email, Teams, and meetings — what does that do to the informal, candid conversations that happen in those channels?
