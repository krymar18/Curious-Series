Copilot in Teams

A practical reference for every major Copilot feature inside Microsoft Teams — what each one does, where it’s reliable, and how to use it in real work.

Objective

Know what Copilot can and cannot do inside Teams — and build the habit of reaching for the right feature at the right moment instead of defaulting to a single workflow for everything.

Key idea

Copilot in Teams is not one tool — it’s a set of distinct features tied to different moments in a meeting or conversation. The value is in knowing which one to use when, not in turning everything on at once.

What You Have

Everyone in this training has Microsoft 365 Copilot — the full license tier. Expand below for the full breakdown of what each tier unlocks, so you understand where the ceiling is for colleagues on lower tiers.

The one rule that applies to almost everything

Transcription must be running for Copilot to work in or after a meeting. No transcript = no recap, no live Q&A, no Facilitator notes. Enable automatic transcription in Teams Admin Center, or start it manually at the beginning of any meeting where you want Copilot features.

Features

Four clusters — grouped by when in your workflow you’d reach for them. Each sub-feature includes what it does, how reliable it is, prompts you can copy directly, and an exercise to try in Teams.

Meeting Record — Recap, Intelligent Recap & Audio Recap

The meeting record features generate automatically after a recorded meeting. They don’t require anything during the call — but the quality of the output depends heavily on how explicitly people spoke.

Standard Meeting Recap M365 Base+

Generates automatically after a transcribed meeting. Produces a text summary, an action item list with attribution, a full transcript, and an attendance report — all stored in the meeting event in Teams Calendar, accessible to all attendees.

Excellent at
  • Capturing explicit decisions and named action items stated aloud
  • Creating a searchable record of who attended and what was said
  • Post-meeting follow-up when you were in the room
Better avoided for
  • Decisions made in the chat tab or via side conversations
  • Implied agreements or non-verbal consensus
  • High-stakes action items where misattribution has real consequences
What it capturesReliabilityWhy
Explicit decisions stated aloudHighDirect statement, speaker-attributed
Named action items with ownerHighClear verb + owner pattern recognised reliably
Deadlines mentioned aloudMediumCaptured if stated clearly, missed if implied
Decisions made in the chat tab simultaneouslyLowRecap does not read the meeting chat thread
Implied consensus or non-verbal agreementNoneAudio only — no visual input
In practice

A 60-second verbal close — “So to recap: we agreed on X, Marcus owns Y by Thursday, and we’ll revisit Z next week” — produces a dramatically better AI summary than a meeting that ends with everyone just dropping off the call.

Post-meeting recap prompt

List every decision made in this meeting. Format as a numbered list. For each decision include: the decision itself, who owns it if stated, and any deadline mentioned.

Exercise — Your First Recap
  1. Schedule a 15-minute test meeting with at least one other person. Before it starts, enable automatic transcription in Meeting Options.
  2. During the meeting, explicitly state three made-up decisions with owners and fake deadlines. Say them out loud clearly.
  3. After the meeting, open the Recap tab in Teams Calendar and compare the AI summary to what was actually said.
  4. Open the Copilot pane and paste the prompt above.
  5. Reflection: What was captured accurately? What was missed or misattributed?
Intelligent Recap Teams Premium+

Builds on standard recap with AI-generated topic chapters, a clickable speaker timeline, and custom summary types. A 90-minute meeting becomes a navigable document — click any chapter heading to jump directly to that moment in the recording.

Excellent at
  • Long or recurring meetings where finding a specific discussion is painful
  • Briefing yourself on what someone specific said or committed to
  • Creating a navigable archive of decisions over time
Better avoided for
  • Forwarding the Executive Report without reading it first
  • Meetings where nuance and subtext matter more than the stated words
  • Short stand-ups under 15 minutes — chapters won’t add value

Speaker Summary

Per-person breakdown: what each attendee said, what they were assigned, and what they asked. Most useful for reviewing a meeting you couldn’t attend — go straight to one person’s contributions instead of reading the full transcript.

Executive Report

A condensed version for stakeholders not in the room — strategic context, key decisions, next steps. Review before forwarding. It can flatten nuanced or unresolved discussions into confident-sounding statements.

Speaker summary prompt

Generate a speaker summary. For each participant: summarise their key contributions, any tasks they were assigned, and any questions they raised that remain open.

Exercise — Intelligent Recap Navigation
  1. Open a completed meeting in Teams Calendar that has an Intelligent Recap.
  2. Review the chapter headings in the AI Summary tab. Do the titles accurately describe each segment?
  3. Click a chapter heading to jump to that moment in the recording — does it land in the right place?
  4. Open Custom Summary → Speaker Summary. Review what was attributed to one person. Is it accurate?
  5. Reflection: For which recurring Ampersand meetings would chapters save the most time?
Audio Recap M365 Copilot

Converts the meeting transcript into a short podcast-style spoken summary — two AI voices discussing the highlights conversationally. Available on desktop and mobile. Designed for catching up on missed meetings when reading isn’t practical.

Excellent at
  • Catching up on a missed meeting during a commute or walk
  • Users who absorb spoken information better than written summaries
Better avoided for
  • Meetings with detailed technical content, figures, or decisions needing verification
  • Situations where you’d naturally forward the summary — it’s audio only

What it is — and what it isn’t

The voices are synthetic and the conversational framing is AI-generated. It’s a performance of a summary, not a recording of the meeting. It does not replace the text recap — it’s an additional format. Some people absorb spoken summaries more effectively; others find the format disorienting. Try it once before deciding whether it’s useful for you.

Live Meeting — Copilot During Calls & Facilitator Agent

These features run during an active meeting. Live Copilot is private — only you see it. Facilitator is visible to the whole group. Both require transcription to be running, and both work best in meetings that are at least 15–20 minutes in.

Live Meeting Copilot M365 Copilot

A private real-time AI assistant in a sidebar during any active Teams meeting. Other participants cannot see your queries or Copilot’s responses. Open it from the sparkle icon in the meeting controls toolbar.

Excellent at
  • Catching up after joining late without interrupting the conversation
  • Checking what action items have been assigned before a meeting closes
  • Surfacing unresolved questions in a complex multi-topic discussion
Better avoided for
  • Meetings under 15 minutes — not enough transcript content to summarise
  • Relying on it for the current moment — it lags 1–2 minutes behind
  • Meetings with heavy crosstalk or multiple simultaneous speakers
What to askExampleBest moment
Catch up on join“I just joined — what have I missed?”Joining late, no need to interrupt the call
Decision check“What decisions have been made so far?”Near the end of the meeting, before closing
Action item scan“What open action items have been mentioned?”30–45 mins into a long meeting
Unresolved tensions“What questions or disagreements remain open?”Complex discussions with multiple positions
Speaker summary“Summarise what [person] has said so far.”Want to follow up on a specific person’s points

Reliability caveat

Live Copilot has a 1–2 minute context lag — it summarises what was said recently, not what’s happening right now. It cannot see shared screens, read the meeting chat, or access pre-meeting documents unless those were also transcribed. Performs poorly in meetings with heavy crosstalk or multiple simultaneous speakers.

Late-join prompt

I just joined — what have I missed? Summarise the key points and any decisions made so far.

Pre-close check prompt

What open questions or unresolved issues remain from this discussion? List each with the person who raised it if clear.

Exercise — Live Copilot in Your Next Meeting
  1. In your next meeting where you join late, open the Copilot pane immediately and use the late-join prompt above.
  2. At the 30-minute mark of a long meeting, ask: “What action items have been discussed so far? Who owns each one?”
  3. Near the end, use the pre-close prompt. Compare its output to your own mental list of what’s unresolved.
  4. Reflection: Did using Copilot during the meeting change how you participated? Were you more or less attentive to the conversation?
Facilitator Agent M365 Copilot

Shifts Copilot from your personal assistant to a meeting-wide AI participant. When the organiser enables Facilitator, it captures highlights and decisions in a shared notes document visible to all participants in real time — and posts updates to the meeting chat as the conversation progresses.

Excellent at
  • Project kickoffs and working sessions where shared notes are expected
  • Reducing the manual note-taking burden on a specific person
  • Creating a real-time shared record for distributed teams
Better avoided for
  • Performance reviews, HR discussions, or sensitive negotiations — shared AI notes change the dynamic
  • Any meeting where participants haven’t been told it’s running

How to enable it

Before the meeting: Meeting Options → Copilot → “During and after the meeting” → enable Allow Facilitator. Or during an active meeting, open the Copilot pane and select “Add to this meeting.”

Facilitator vs. personal Copilot

Personal Copilot answers your private questions. Facilitator assists the group. Both can run simultaneously — but Facilitator’s output is shared with everyone in the meeting, so what it captures is visible to all participants.

Disclose it before you use it

When Facilitator is active, participants should know. In performance reviews, HR discussions, or client negotiations, shared AI note-taking changes how people speak. State it at the start of the meeting — “I’m running Copilot Facilitator, which is taking shared notes for all of us” — before enabling it.

Chat & Channels — Summarisation, @Copilot & Message Drafting

These features work outside of meetings — in your ongoing Teams chats and channels. They reduce the time it takes to get up to speed on a thread, and help you draft messages without starting from a blank compose box.

Chat & Channel Summarisation M365 Base+

In any 1:1 or group chat, open the Copilot sidebar (icon, top right) to summarise the last 30 days of conversation. In channels, Copilot can summarise a long thread before you reply — useful for avoiding the classic mistake of asking a question that was answered three days ago.

Excellent at
  • Getting up to speed on a chat thread you’ve been away from
  • Identifying what was decided or agreed across a long back-and-forth
  • Checking who said what before adding your reply to a channel thread
Better avoided for
  • Decisions made in shared files or attachments — Copilot cannot read them
  • Tracking commitments made via emoji reactions or link-only messages

What Copilot cannot read in chat

Files attached to messages, links (only the link text, not the destination page), Loop components, images, and emoji reactions are all excluded from summaries. Decisions made in a shared document won’t be captured unless they were also stated in the chat text itself.

Chat decisions prompt

What decisions or agreements were made in this chat over the last two weeks? List them with the date and who made each decision.

Channel thread prompt

Summarise this channel thread. What is the main topic being discussed? What decisions or conclusions have been reached, if any? What questions remain open?

Exercise — Chat Summarisation
  1. Open a Teams group chat that’s been active in the last 30 days.
  2. Open the Copilot pane and use the decisions prompt above.
  3. Click one of the sources Copilot cites — does it scroll to the correct message?
  4. Find a channel thread with 10+ replies. Use the thread prompt before adding your reply.
  5. Reflection: What context did the summary miss that you knew from being in the conversation?
@Copilot in Group Chats M365 Copilot

Copilot can be added as a participant to any Teams group chat. Once added, any member can @mention it and its response is posted to the whole group. To add it: click “Add people” → search for Copilot, or type @Copilot directly in the compose box to invoke it.

Excellent at
  • Summarising the thread for a new member joining a group chat mid-project
  • Drafting a meeting agenda from open topics the group has been discussing
  • Answering shared questions where the whole group benefits from the answer
Better avoided for
  • Questions that reveal what you don’t know — the whole group sees both question and answer
  • Sensitive topics where the AI response visible to all participants could create issues

This is public, not private

When you @Copilot in a group chat, everyone sees both the question and the answer. Before tagging it, consider whether your question — or what it might reveal about what you don’t know — is appropriate for all participants to see.

Message Compose & Reply Assist M365 Copilot

In any Teams chat or channel, click the Copilot icon in the compose box to draft a message from a plain-language description. Choose from six tone options before accepting. For replies, right-click any message and select “Reply with Copilot” to generate a contextual draft response.

Excellent at
  • Breaking out of default tone — seeing the same message in six registers is genuinely useful
  • First drafts for announcements, updates, or requests where you know what to say but not how
  • Quick professional replies to messages you’re unsure how to word
Better avoided for
  • Messages requiring relationship context Copilot doesn’t have
  • Sending without personalising — Copilot-drafted replies that are wrong are worse than no reply
ToneBest used for
CasualQuick updates to teammates you work with closely
ProfessionalStakeholder communications, formal requests, escalations
ConfidentRecommendations, pushing back on decisions, requests for action
EnthusiasticAnnouncements, wins, recognition messages
InformationalStatus updates, process instructions, documentation
ConciseBusy senior stakeholders, quick confirmations, short updates
Compose example

Draft a message to my team letting them know the weekly operations review is moving from Tuesday to Thursday this week, and that everyone needs to review the attached fulfillment report beforehand. Professional but not stiff.

Exercise — Message Drafting
  1. Think of a real message you need to send this week — an update, a request, a piece of news.
  2. Use Copilot compose. Describe what you want to say in plain language — not how to say it.
  3. Generate the message in at least three different tones. Compare them side by side.
  4. Choose the closest version, edit it, and send.
  5. Reflection: Did Copilot produce something you wouldn’t have written yourself? Was that better or worse?
Beyond Teams — Scheduling, Cross-App Actions & Language

These features extend Copilot beyond a single meeting or chat thread. Cross-app actions turn a meeting transcript into a working document, email draft, or slide deck. Scheduling removes coordination overhead. Language features make multilingual meetings more accessible to all participants.

Cross-App Actions M365 Copilot

From the Recap tab of any transcribed meeting, you can ask Copilot to produce documents in other M365 apps without leaving Teams. The meeting record becomes the starting point for downstream communication and project work.

Excellent at
  • Producing a follow-up email, action item table, or status doc directly from a meeting transcript
  • Removing the post-meeting gap between “meeting ended” and “follow-up sent”
  • Generating a first draft that you edit rather than write from scratch
Better avoided for
  • Final client-facing outputs sent without review — attribution errors in a recap become attribution errors in the email
  • Meetings where the key context was in a shared screen or document, not the audio

Post-meeting follow-up email prompt

Using the transcript from this meeting, draft an email to the attendees summarising: (1) the three key decisions made, (2) each person’s action item and deadline, (3) the date of our next check-in. Keep the tone professional and the length under 200 words.

Exercise — Post-Meeting Cross-App
  1. After your next recorded meeting, open the Recap tab and use the follow-up email prompt above.
  2. Review the draft — does it accurately capture the decisions and action items?
  3. Edit before sending. Note what you had to change.
  4. Bonus: Ask Copilot to generate a table of action items from the same transcript, then paste it into Excel.
  5. Reflection: What part of your post-meeting workflow could this reliably handle? What still requires human judgment?
Scheduling from Chat M365 Copilot

From inside a Teams chat, Copilot can find shared availability, create calendar invites, draft meeting agendas, and book rooms — without leaving the conversation.

Excellent at
  • Finding shared availability and drafting an agenda in one step, without switching apps
  • Turning a chat discussion directly into a scheduled follow-up meeting
Better avoided for
  • Room bookings — depends on Exchange configuration and frequently fails
  • Meetings with external guests — availability lookups break for people outside your tenant

Where it’s unreliable

Room booking depends on your organisation’s room calendars being configured in Exchange — the most inconsistent part of this feature. Availability lookups work for internal attendees but break for external guests. Copilot can schedule but cannot re-schedule or cancel without confirmation. Treat it as a fast first step that you verify before sending.

Scheduling prompt

Find a time next week when I’m free for a 30-minute meeting with [colleague name]. Draft a 3-item agenda based on what we’ve discussed in this chat. Send the invite with the agenda attached.

Ampersand Spotlight — Using This Responsibly

Copilot in Teams records, stores, and makes searchable what people say in meetings. That changes how the tool should be deployed — not whether to deploy it.

Disclose transcription before the meeting starts

Teams shows a notification when transcription starts, but it’s easy to miss. State it verbally: “I’m recording and transcribing this meeting with Copilot — the summary will be accessible to all attendees afterward.” In some jurisdictions, recording without explicit consent of all participants has legal implications that Microsoft’s platform does not manage for you.

Some meeting types shouldn’t be transcribed by default

Performance reviews, sensitive HR conversations, and active negotiations change in character when participants know their words are being stored in a searchable database. Define which meeting types require opt-in transcription rather than automatic recording — and communicate that standard to the team before it becomes an issue.

Review recaps before forwarding them

A well-formatted AI summary creates an impression of accuracy the underlying model doesn’t always support. Recaps can misattribute ideas, present deferred decisions as resolved, or drop the condition attached to an action item. Read it before forwarding it anywhere — especially to stakeholders who weren’t in the room.

Your data stays in your M365 tenant

Microsoft’s position: prompts, responses, and meeting content processed by M365 Copilot remain within your Microsoft 365 service boundary and are not used to train foundational models. Transcript retention is configurable by admin — default varies by tenant policy, typically 60–180 days. Users can delete Copilot activity history via the My Account portal.

External guests need to know too

If Ampersand hosts a meeting with external guests, those participants should be told the meeting is being transcribed and summarised by AI — before the meeting begins, not at the end. This is both courtesy and, in some US states and EU countries, a legal obligation.

Persistent recording changes how people speak

Knowing that meetings are summarised and stored affects candour in brainstorming, willingness to commit to things in early-stage discussions, and how people handle disagreement on the record. This isn’t a reason to avoid Copilot — it’s a reason to think about meeting culture alongside the tool rollout, not after it.

Conclusion & Reflections

Take a moment to reflect — it’s what makes the learning stick.

Key Takeaways
  • Copilot in Teams is not one tool — it’s a set of features with different triggers, behaviours, and reliability levels.
  • Almost everything depends on transcription being on. That’s the prerequisite, not an afterthought.
  • Recap quality reflects how explicitly people spoke. Vague meetings produce vague summaries.
  • Live Copilot lags 1–2 minutes and can’t see screens or chat. It’s a catch-up tool, not a real-time scribe.
  • AI summaries look authoritative. Read them before forwarding — they can misattribute and flatten nuance.
  • Cross-app actions are where the real leverage is: turning a meeting into a draft email, doc, or deck before you close your laptop.
Reflection
  • Which part of your current meeting workflow costs you the most time — prep, follow-up, catching up, or briefing others?
  • If Copilot generates a summary of your meetings anyway, what does that change about what you need to accomplish in the room?
  • Have you ever forwarded a meeting summary without reading it? What was at stake?
  • Which meeting types at Ampersand should not be transcribed by default — and do you have a standard for that today?
