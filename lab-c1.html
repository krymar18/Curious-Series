Search SharePoint by Meaning, Not Keyword

Find a document you need using intent rather than exact filename — then ask a specific question of it and verify the answer against the original source.

8 – 10 min Copilot Chat with work data enabled M365 Copilot Premium
1
Find the document by meaning
Copilot Chat
▶
Do this
Open Copilot Chat (copilot.microsoft.com). Think of a document you know exists on SharePoint but cannot easily find by filename. Replace the brackets with the actual topic.
Prompt 1 — Find by meaning

Find the most recent document on [topic or subject] in our SharePoint. I do not know the exact filename. Return: document title, who created it, date, and a one-sentence summary of what it covers.

◉
Look for
Did Copilot find the right document? If not, rephrase in plain language rather than trying to match vocabulary you think the document uses. Plain language outperforms technical vocabulary in semantic search.
2
Ask a specific question of the document
Copilot Chat
▶
Do this
Once Copilot surfaces a document, ask a targeted question of it:
Prompt 2 — Document Q&A

Based on that document: what is [your specific question]? Quote the relevant passage alongside your answer. If the document does not address it, say so explicitly — do not infer from nearby content.

◉
Look for
Did Copilot quote the passage or paraphrase it? A quote you can verify. A paraphrase you cannot. For high-stakes questions, always ask for the quote.
3
Verify against the original
SharePoint
▶
Do this
Open the original document. Find the passage Copilot quoted. Read it in full context. Does the quote mean what Copilot said it means?
Key Takeaways
  • Semantic search finds content by intent — plain language finds the right document even when your words do not match the document's vocabulary.
  • Always ask Copilot to quote the passage alongside its answer. If it cannot quote it, the answer may not be in the document — it may be a plausible inference.
  • Rephrasing a search query changes the results. If the first result is wrong, rephrase rather than trying to match the document's vocabulary.
