Group and Count Themes Across Multiple Items

Paste a set of similar items and ask Copilot to group them by theme with counts — then challenge the most prominent pattern to test whether it is signal or coincidence.

10 – 12 min 5–15 similar text items: notes, responses, or records M365 Copilot Premium

Gather and paste your items
Copilot Chat
▶
Do this
Gather 5–15 similar items of text — call notes, survey responses, client feedback, error messages. Number them 1 to N and paste them into Copilot Chat.
Prompt 1 — Group and count

Group the following [call notes / responses / records] by theme. For each theme: (1) a label, (2) how many items fall into it, (3) one representative example. List themes by frequency, highest first.

◉
Look for
Notice the most common theme. Does it reflect what you expected, or is it a surprise? A surprise is worth investigating; confirmation of what you already knew is worth questioning.
2
Challenge the top pattern

▶

Ask Copilot to test whether the top theme is a real signal:
Prompt 2 — Challenge the pattern

For the most common theme you identified: what alternative explanation could produce the same pattern in this data? Is this a real signal or could it be coincidental clustering?

◈
Reflect
Would you have found any of these themes reading manually? How long would that have taken? Which theme is most actionable and what would you do with it?
Key Takeaways
  • AI will find a pattern in almost any dataset — the pattern is real in the text but may not be real in the world. Always ask for the alternative explanation.
  • "Group and count" produces more actionable output than "what are the themes?" — the count forces a ranking that tells you where to focus first.
  • The most useful insight is usually the theme you did not expect, not the one that confirms what you already thought.
