Lab A — Teams

From Meeting to Communication

A real Teams meeting happened. Copilot was there. This lab walks you through using Copilot to extract everything useful from it — then turn it into the documentation, follow-ups, and audience-adapted communications that should come out of every meeting but rarely do.

Time required
30 – 45 minutes
What you need
A recorded Teams meeting with a transcript
Licence required
Microsoft 365 Copilot (Premium)

Before you start

Find a Teams meeting that was recorded in the last two weeks. It should have at least three participants and cover a real project or topic — the more substantive the meeting, the more useful this lab will be. Open it in Teams before you begin. If you cannot find a recorded meeting, ask your manager or team lead if there is a recent one you can use.

Part 1

Reading the Meeting

1
Get the plain-language summary
Teams · Copilot
Do this
Open the meeting in Teams. Click the Recap tab, then open Copilot. Paste the prompt below.
Copy prompt

Summarise this meeting in plain language. Structure it as: (1) Purpose — what was the meeting for? (2) Key decisions made. (3) Key information shared. (4) What was not resolved or left open. Keep each section to 3 sentences maximum.

What to look for
Does the summary capture what you remember the meeting being about? Is the "not resolved" section accurate, or is it empty when it should not be? Notice how long it took Copilot to produce this vs. how long it would have taken you to write it manually.
Reflect
Did Copilot miss anything you consider significant? If so, what type of thing — a decision, a concern someone raised, a commitment someone made? That gap tells you something about where you still need to be the note-taker.
2
Extract action items with owners
Teams · Copilot
Do this
In the same Copilot thread, send the next prompt. Do not open a new chat — Copilot already has context from Step 1.
Copy prompt

List every action item from this meeting. For each one: (1) the action, (2) who owns it by name, (3) any deadline mentioned, (4) whether the person explicitly agreed to it or it was assigned to them. If no deadline was mentioned, say so.

What to look for
Check whether actions are attributed to the right people — misattribution is the most common error in AI meeting notes. Also note the distinction Copilot makes between items that were explicitly agreed vs. assigned — that difference matters when following up.
Ethics checkpoint

Before sharing AI-generated action items with the people they name: read every item. An incorrect attribution sent to a colleague creates a paper trail with wrong information in it. You are responsible for what you forward — AI-generated does not mean reviewed.

3
Q&A — ask specific questions of the transcript
Teams · Copilot
Do this
Pick one topic, decision, or person from your meeting summary and ask a specific question about it. Use the example below as a template, replacing the bracketed parts with something real from your meeting.
Copy prompt — adapt to your meeting

What exactly did [name or topic] say about [specific subject]? Quote the relevant part of the transcript. If they said nothing about it, say so rather than summarising nearby content.

Follow-up prompt

Was there any pushback or disagreement on this in the meeting? If so, who raised it and what was their concern?

What to look for
Notice whether Copilot quotes the transcript or paraphrases. A quote you can verify. A paraphrase you cannot — it may be accurate or it may be a close approximation. For high-stakes questions, always ask for the quote. If Copilot cannot quote it, the answer may not be in the transcript.
4
Find related emails on the same project
Copilot Chat · SharePoint
Do this
Open Copilot Chat (copilot.microsoft.com or the Copilot app). Use the prompt below to search across your emails and SharePoint for content related to the same project or topic as your meeting. Replace the brackets with the actual project name or topic.
Copy prompt

Find emails and documents related to [project name or topic] from the last 30 days. For each one: who sent it or created it, the date, and a one-sentence summary of what it covers. Flag any that contain decisions or commitments that were not mentioned in my Teams meeting today.

SharePoint-specific prompt

Search SharePoint for any documents or pages about [project name or topic]. Summarise what each one covers and highlight anything that might contradict or add to what was discussed in my recent meeting.

What to look for
Are there decisions made over email that contradict what was decided in the meeting? Is there a document on SharePoint that should have been discussed but wasn't? This cross-referencing step is where Copilot's access across M365 adds real value — a single prompt covers emails, chats, and SharePoint simultaneously.
Reflect
Did Copilot surface anything you had forgotten or were not aware of? If there were contradictions between the email record and the meeting discussion — which one reflects the current agreed position?
Ethics checkpoint

Copilot Chat with work data accesses emails and documents shared with you within your organisation's M365 tenancy. It cannot access content from other tenancies, personal email accounts, or content you do not have permission to view. Be aware of what you are asking it to surface — particularly if the meeting or project involves sensitive HR, financial, or legal content.

5
Adjust the summary using what you found
Teams · Copilot
Do this
Go back to your Teams Copilot thread. If you found anything in Step 4 that adds context or changes the picture — a prior decision, an outstanding document, a conflicting commitment — paste it in and ask Copilot to revise the summary.
Copy prompt

Revise the meeting summary from earlier. Add the following context that was not in the transcript: [paste the relevant detail from Step 4]. Update the 'not resolved' section if this changes what is still open. Keep the same structure.

Part 1 checkpoint — before moving on, confirm:
  • You have a summary you would be comfortable sharing with someone who missed the meeting
  • Action items are attributed to the correct people
  • The open items section reflects what you know to still be unresolved
  • You have cross-referenced with relevant emails or SharePoint documents
Part 2

Writing from the Meeting

6
Draft a Teams channel follow-up
Teams · Copilot
Do this
Still in your Teams Copilot thread. Ask Copilot to draft a channel message using the summary it has already built. Specify the audience — your immediate team — and keep it short.
Copy prompt

Using the meeting summary above, draft a Teams channel message for the project team. It should cover: what was decided, who owns what next step, and what is still open. Audience: people who were in the meeting. Tone: direct and operational, not formal. Under 150 words.

What to look for
Does it read like something you would actually send? Or does it still feel like AI wrote it — filler language, over-formal, or missing your team's normal register? That gap is what a manual editing pass closes.
7
Transform it — rewrite for a different audience
Teams · Copilot
Do this
Now ask Copilot to rewrite the same information as a formal internal memo — for someone senior who was not in the meeting and needs to understand the outcome without context.
Copy prompt

Rewrite the meeting summary as a formal internal memo. Audience: a senior stakeholder who was not in the meeting and has no prior context on this project. Format: Subject, Background (2 sentences max), Decisions Made, Next Steps with owners, Open Items. Tone: professional and precise. Under 200 words.

Reflect on the difference
Compare the channel message (Step 6) and the memo (Step 7) side by side. The facts are the same. What changed? Vocabulary, structure, assumed knowledge, level of formality. This is exactly what "transforming text" means — same content, different register. How long would this have taken to produce manually?
8
Rewrite as a one-paragraph Slack or email update
Teams · Copilot
Do this
One more transformation — this time for a partner or external-adjacent colleague who needs a brief update but not the full detail.
Copy prompt

Condense the meeting summary into a single paragraph update for someone adjacent to the project — not a direct team member, but someone who needs to stay informed. Include only: the key outcome, the most important next step, and whether anything is blocked. Maximum 60 words.

Part 2 checkpoint — you now have three versions:
  • A team channel message (operational, short, direct)
  • A formal memo (structured, precise, context-included)
  • A one-paragraph update (concise, outcome-focused)
Part 3

Ethics and Quality Check

9
What should not have been summarised
Reflection
Ethics — before you share anything
  • Confidential or sensitive discussion. If the meeting included HR matters, personal performance feedback, legal discussions, or financial detail not intended for broad circulation — that content should not appear in a summary you forward. AI does not redact; you do.
  • Opinions and tone. AI transcription captures what was said but loses how it was said. A comment that was clearly meant as a joke, sarcastic, or hypothetical may appear in a summary as a statement of fact or intent. Read everything before it goes out.
  • Attribution of disagreement. If Copilot noted that someone raised an objection, confirm this is accurate and that the person is comfortable with it being in writing before distributing. Putting pushback in a document without consent changes the dynamic.
  • AI-generated ≠ verified. You are responsible for the accuracy of what you send. "Copilot wrote it" is not a defence for incorrect meeting notes.
Reflect
Go back through your three outputs. Is there anything in them that should not be in writing? Anything that needs to be softened, attributed more carefully, or removed entirely before sharing? Make those edits now — before considering these drafts final.
10
Ask Copilot what it missed
Teams · Copilot
Do this
Return to your Teams Copilot thread one more time. Ask it directly what it may have missed or handled poorly.
Copy prompt

Looking at the summary and outputs you have produced from this meeting: what are you least confident about? Where might the transcript have been unclear, incomplete, or where might your summary have simplified something that was more nuanced? Tell me where I should double-check against the original recording.

What to look for
Does Copilot identify real gaps — moments where multiple people spoke at once, technical terms that may have been transcribed incorrectly, sections where tone was ambiguous? This self-critique prompt is one of the most useful habits you can build.
Wrap-up

Final Reflections

What you practised
  • Reading a meeting: plain-language summary, action items, open items, Q&A
  • Cross-referencing with emails and SharePoint to complete the picture
  • Adjusting the summary based on what you found elsewhere
  • Transforming the same content for three different audiences and registers
  • Ethics review before sharing anything AI-generated
  • Asking Copilot to surface its own uncertainty
Questions to take away
  • How long would this have taken without Copilot? Where did it save the most time?
  • Which step required the most manual review or correction?
  • Was there anything in the outputs you would not have been comfortable sharing without editing?
  • What would need to change about how your team runs meetings to make this workflow smoother?
  • What habit from this lab would be worth doing after every significant meeting?
