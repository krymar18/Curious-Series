Working with Context

This section turns the earlier concepts into a practical prompting toolkit: context, persona, strategy selection, iterative refinement, and the habit of saving what works.

Core message

AI starts from zero each session. Vague prompts get vague answers. Most useful outputs come from two or three rounds of feedback, not one perfect prompt.

Prompt basics

What do you want it to do? Who is it for? What format do you want? What tone is right? What should it avoid?

Context Is Everything

AI starts from zero every session. It knows nothing about you, your work, your industry, or what a good outcome looks like — unless you tell it.

AI Starts From Zero
Think through metaphor

Using AI is like directing a play. You've cast the best actor in the world — someone who can genuinely play anyone. You walk up to them and say: "You're a doctor." The chances of getting what you want are low. Are they a villain or a hero? Mid-emergency in an ER, or having a quiet conversation with their family? Even the most talented actor needs to know who they are, where they are, and what they're trying to do in this scene.

Vague prompts get vague answers. Most good outputs come from two or three rounds of feedback, not one perfect prompt.

What Goes Into a Good Prompt
  • What do you want the AI to do?
  • Who is it for?
  • What format do you want?
  • What tone is right?
  • What should it avoid?
Copy prompt starter

I need you to [task]. This is for [audience]. Format it as [format]. The tone should be [tone]. Avoid [anything to exclude].

Giving AI a Persona

A persona is simply a starting point you define before you ask anything. A basic persona answers three things:

Question What it means Weak version Strong version
Who are you? A role with enough specificity to matter An assistant A senior HR manager handling internal communications
Where are you? The situation you're operating in At work Responding to a client complaint after a missed deadline
What are you trying to do? The goal behind the task, not just the task Write an email Write an email that keeps the relationship intact while still saying no

In practice

You don't need a perfect prompt to start. Give the AI a role, a situation, and a goal — then refine from there. Two or three rounds of feedback will get you further than one carefully constructed prompt.

Prompt strategies

The strategies are grouped by what they do, not by difficulty level. Use the sections below as a quick reference.

Group 1 — How much guidance you give the AI
  • Zero-shot prompting: Ask directly with no examples. Fast, but less controlled.
  • Few-shot prompting: Give 2–5 examples of the output you want. Helpful when format matters.

Use zero-shot when the task is clear. Use few-shot when you need a specific structure or pattern repeated reliably.

Group 2 — How you frame the request
  • Direct instruction prompting: Tell the AI exactly what to do.
  • Question-based prompting: Frame the request as a question.
  • Open-ended prompting: Leave the prompt broad and exploratory.
Group 3 — How you control the reasoning process
  • Step-by-step instruction prompting: Work through tasks in explicit sequence.
  • Chain-of-thought prompting: Ask the AI to show its reasoning at each step.
  • Prompt chaining: Break a complex task into smaller prompts that build on one another.
  • Task decomposition prompting: Build the decomposition into a single prompt instead of separate turns.
Group 4 — How you set identity and perspective
  • Role-based prompting: Assign the AI a professional role.
  • Persona-based prompting: Add personality, values, and communication style.
  • Complex persona prompting: Use a full character brief with background, constraints, style, and goals.
  • Multiple personas prompting: Run the same task through several distinct personas at once.
Group 5 — How you refine and recover
  • Iterative prompting: Follow up with corrections and refinements.
  • Confirmatory prompting: Ask the AI to confirm its understanding before it answers.
  • Negative prompting: Tell the AI what to avoid.
  • Self-reflection prompting: Ask the AI to critique and revise its own output.
  • Fallback prompting: Build fallback alternatives into the prompt in case the primary request fails.
Group 6 — How you load context
  • Contextual prompting: Add background information directly into the prompt.
  • Context expansion prompting: Gradually build context across a conversation.
  • Scenario-based prompting: Frame the task inside a realistic situation.

How to choose a strategy

  1. How defined is the output I need? If you know exactly what you want, lean toward direct instruction or few-shot. If you're exploring, open-ended or scenario-based gives the AI more room.
  2. How complex is the task? Single, clear request — one strategy, one prompt. Multi-part task — prompt chaining or task decomposition.
  3. How much do I trust the first output? High-stakes — build in a checkpoint. Routine task — iterate quickly instead.

Can you combine strategies? Yes. A persona sets the frame. Direct instruction defines the task. Negative prompting removes what you don't want. They stack.

Prompt Constructor

Use a task from your own work — or load one of the samples to explore the format.

Load a sample:
Role *
Who is the AI? Define the expertise, domain, and seniority it should adopt. The more specific the lens, the more calibrated the output.

Example: "You are a senior cable TV account executive with 10 years in local market sales."
Context & Background *
What does the AI need to know first? Audience, situation, prior decisions — anything it cannot infer from the task alone. Think: what would a smart new colleague need on day one?

Example: "I'm preparing a pacing report for a national auto client whose campaign is 15% behind delivery. The audience is our sales lead, not the client directly."
Tasks *
What do you want the AI to produce? Use a specific verb — Summarise, Draft, Evaluate, Identify. One operation per row, in the order they need to happen.

Row 1: "Summarise the top 3 delivery risks with supporting figures."
Row 2: "Draft a 3-sentence client-facing note for each risk."
Constraints
Rules the AI must follow. Three types: Format (structure, length), Tone (voice, register), Content (what to avoid or not claim). Add each rule as its own row.

Examples: "No jargon." / "Under 200 words." / "Bullet points only." / "Do not mention competitor names."

Key Takeaways
  • AI starts from zero every session — your context is its only starting point.
  • A vague prompt gets a vague answer. Role, situation, and goal are the minimum.
  • Most good outputs come from iteration, not one perfect prompt.
Reflection
  • What was the first thing you changed about the bad text? What does that tell you about what you value in writing?
  • What prompt got you the closest to what you actually wanted? Why do you think it worked?
