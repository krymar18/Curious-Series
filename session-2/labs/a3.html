Cross-Reference a Meeting with Email and SharePoint

Find emails and SharePoint documents related to the same project as your meeting, and identify anything that contradicts or adds to what was discussed in the room.

10 – 12 min Copilot Chat with work data enabled M365 Copilot Premium
1
Search email and documents
Copilot Chat
▶
Do this
Open Copilot Chat (copilot.microsoft.com or the Copilot app). Replace the brackets with the actual project name or topic from your meeting.
Copy prompt — Email and docs

Find emails and documents related to [project name or topic] from the last 30 days. For each: who sent or created it, the date, and a one-sentence summary. Flag any that contain decisions or commitments not mentioned in my recent Teams meeting.

2
Search SharePoint specifically
Copilot Chat
▶
Do this
Follow up with a SharePoint-targeted search in the same thread:
Follow-up — SharePoint

Search SharePoint for any documents or pages about [project name or topic]. Summarise what each covers and highlight anything that might contradict or add to what was discussed in my meeting.

◉
Look for
Are there decisions made over email that contradict what was decided in the meeting? Is there a SharePoint document that should have been discussed but was not? This cross-referencing step is where Copilot's access across M365 adds real value.
◈
Reflect
If there are contradictions between the email record and the meeting discussion — which source reflects the current agreed position? That answer determines what your meeting summary should say.
⚑ Ethics

Copilot Chat only accesses content within your organisation's M365 tenancy that you have permission to view. It cannot access other tenancies, personal email, or restricted content. Be aware of what you are surfacing if the project involves HR, legal, or financial content.

Key Takeaways
  • A single Copilot Chat prompt searches emails, Teams chats, and SharePoint simultaneously — a cross-reference that would take 15 minutes manually takes under a minute.
  • Contradictions between the email record and a meeting discussion are common. Finding them before acting on meeting notes is the entire point of this step.
  • Cross-referencing is not about distrust — it is about completeness. Decisions made in a meeting without the email context are frequently incomplete.
