Read a Thread Completely in 10 Minutes

Summarise a thread, surface the unanswered questions and dropped commitments, and identify who needs to act next — without reading every email.

8 – 10 min An email thread with 5+ messages M365 Copilot Premium
1
Summarise the thread
Outlook · Copilot
▶
Do this
Open the thread in Outlook. Click the Copilot button in the toolbar (top right of the reading pane). Run both prompts in the same thread — do not open a new chat.
Prompt 1 — Summary

Summarise this email thread. I want to know: (1) What started this conversation? (2) What has been agreed or decided so far. (3) What is still unresolved or has not been responded to. (4) Who has been most active and what is each person's apparent position. Keep each section to 3 sentences maximum.

◉
Look for
Does Copilot accurately represent each person's position, or does it flatten everyone into a single neutral narrative? Check whether the "unresolved" section reflects what you know to still be open.
2
Surface what nobody has answered
Outlook · Copilot
▶
Do this
Stay in the same thread. Send the second prompt:
Prompt 2 — Find the gaps

What direct questions were asked in this thread that have not been answered? What commitments were made that have no follow-up? What topic was raised once and then dropped without resolution? List each one with the name of the person who raised it.

◈
Reflect
Were any of the unanswered questions raised by you? Are any of the dropped commitments yours? This prompt is most useful as a self-check, not just a tool for tracking other people's follow-through.
Key Takeaways
  • Reading a thread with Copilot is a verification task — your job is to check the output, not accept it. Copilot can misread who said what.
  • The most important unanswered question in any thread is usually the one you forgot you raised. Run the gaps prompt as a self-check before replying.
  • A thread summary without an "unresolved" section is incomplete. If Copilot does not include one, ask for it explicitly.
