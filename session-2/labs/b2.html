Draft, Critique, and Fix a Reply

Write your bullet points, let Copilot write the prose, critique it from the recipient's perspective, and fix one specific problem — all in the same session before sending.

10 – 12 min An email thread you need to respond to M365 Copilot Premium
1
Write your bullet points first
You
▶
Do this
Before touching Copilot: write 3–5 bullet points covering what you need to say, the tone you want, and what to avoid. The substance is yours — Copilot writes the prose.
2
Draft from your bullets
Outlook · Copilot
▶
Do this
Open Copilot in the email thread. Fill in the brackets with your actual bullet points, then send:
Prompt 1 — Draft

Draft a reply to this email thread from my perspective. Here is what I want to communicate: [your bullet points]. Tone: [direct / warm / formal — choose one]. Do not add facts I have not provided. Do not apologise for anything I have not asked you to apologise for. Keep it under 120 words.

◉
Look for
Did Copilot add anything you did not give it — a commitment, an apology, a softening clause? Every word it added that you did not supply is your responsibility if you send it.
3
Run coaching mode
Outlook · Copilot
▶
Do this
Still in the same thread. Ask Copilot to critique what it just wrote — from the recipient's perspective:
Prompt 2 — Coaching

Now read this draft as the recipient — not as a writer. What is the most likely objection or concern they will have after reading it? What is the weakest part — tone, clarity, or what it asks them to do? What one change would make it more likely to get the response I need? Be specific, not diplomatic.

◈
Reflect
Did the coaching output identify something you already knew was weak but were hoping to get away with? That is the most common result — and the most useful one.
4
Fix one specific thing
Outlook · Copilot
▶
Do this
Pick one specific problem from the coaching output. Not "make it better" — one exact thing. Replace the brackets and send:
Prompt 3 — Targeted fix

Rewrite only [the specific weak part Copilot identified]. Keep everything else the same. The fix should address [the specific issue — e.g. the ask is unclear / the tone implies blame / the opening is too formal]. Show me just the rewritten section, not the whole email.

✓ Before you move on
  • Does it sound like you, or like a corporate AI assistant?
  • Is every claim in it accurate and something you are willing to stand behind?
  • Is the ask clear — does the recipient know exactly what you need from them?
Key Takeaways
  • Bullet points first, prose second. A broad prompt produces fluent text where Copilot made the substantive decisions. Your bullet points keep those decisions yours.
  • Coaching mode from the recipient's perspective is the most useful critique you can get before sending anything important.
  • One specific fix beats a full regeneration. Regenerating produces a different email, not necessarily a better one.
