Lab B4 · Outlook

Turn a Thread into a Meeting — Invite and Pre-read

Decide whether a meeting is actually needed, draft a decision-framed invite, and produce a one-page pre-read so attendees arrive with shared context instead of starting from zero.

10 – 12 min An email thread with an unresolved decision M365 Copilot Premium
1
Decide if a meeting is needed
Outlook · Copilot
▶
Do this
Open Copilot in the thread. Start by asking whether a meeting is warranted — most meetings are scheduled before this question is answered.
Prompt 1 — Is a meeting needed?

Based on this email thread: does this need a meeting, or can it be resolved over email? Give me the case for each option. If a meeting is the right call, what is the one question that meeting needs to answer? Who needs to be in the room and who does not?

◈
Reflect
Can you state in one sentence what decision needs to come out of this meeting? If you cannot, you may not be ready to hold it yet.
2
Draft the meeting invite
Outlook · Copilot
▶
Do this
If a meeting is warranted, ask Copilot to draft the invite in the same thread:
Prompt 2 — Meeting invite

Draft a meeting invite based on this thread. Include: (1) a subject line that states the decision to be made — not just the topic, (2) a 2-sentence context section for people who have not read the full thread, (3) the specific question the meeting needs to answer, (4) what each attendee should prepare or bring, (5) what a successful outcome looks like. Under 150 words total.

◉
Look for
Is the subject line a decision ("Approve Q3 budget allocation") or a topic ("Q3 budget")? Decision-framed invites produce better meetings. Do attendees know what to prepare? Without that, most arrive unready to decide anything.
3
Draft the pre-read
Outlook · Copilot
▶
Do this
A pre-read means the meeting starts from shared context. Ask Copilot to write it in the same thread:
Prompt 3 — Pre-read

Write a one-page pre-read for the meeting we just drafted an invite for. Cover: background (what started this), what has been tried or decided so far, what is still unresolved, and the specific question the meeting needs to answer. Written for someone who has not read any of the email thread. Plain language. No jargon.

⚑ Ethics

Meeting invites are commitments — yours and your attendees'. Review the attendee list, the ask, and the pre-read before sending. A poorly framed invite sent at scale is one of the most common sources of wasted time in organisations.

Key Takeaways
  • Most meetings are scheduled before the question they need to answer has been defined. A decision-framed subject line forces that definition before invitations go out.
  • A pre-read replaces the first 10 minutes of context-setting that starts every meeting where nobody read anything in advance.
  • The meeting invite Copilot drafts is a starting point. Review the attendee list, the pre-work ask, and the pre-read before it leaves your drafts.
