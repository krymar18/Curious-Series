Lab C4 · Analysis

Find the Anomaly in Your Data

Define your baseline explicitly, then ask Copilot to flag anything outside it — and ask it to distinguish between noise and a signal worth investigating.

10 – 12 min A dataset or table you work with regularly M365 Copilot Premium
1
Define your baseline first
Copilot Chat
▶
Do this
Take a dataset or table you work with regularly. If it is sensitive: share only column headers and a description of the structure rather than pasting the data. Write down your expected range for the key metric before opening Copilot — this is the most important step.
Prompt 1 — Define and flag

Here is a dataset: [paste or describe]. My expected range for [key metric] is [define your baseline — e.g. within 15% of the 90-day average, between X and Y]. Flag every row that falls outside this range. For each: the value, how far outside the range it is, and whether it is higher or lower than expected.

◉
Look for
How many anomalies were flagged? If there are too many, your baseline definition is too narrow. If there are none, it may be too broad — or your data may genuinely be clean.
2
Distinguish signal from noise
Copilot Chat
▶
Do this
Ask Copilot to rank the anomalies by how likely they are to be real:
Prompt 2 — Signal or noise?

For each anomaly you flagged: what is the most likely innocent explanation — data entry error, seasonal pattern, one-off event? Which anomalies are worth investigating further and which are probably noise? Rank them by how likely they are to reflect a real issue.

3
Pick one to investigate
You
▶
Do this
Pick the top-ranked anomaly. What would you need to check to confirm whether it is a real problem? Who would need to know?
Key Takeaways
  • Anomaly detection without a defined baseline is unreliable. "Find the anomalies" lets Copilot define normal — which may not match your operational reality.
  • AI surfaces candidates for your judgment — it does not make the call. The distinction between signal and noise is yours, not Copilot's.
  • For large or sensitive datasets: share column headers and a description, ask Copilot to write the analysis code, and run it locally.
