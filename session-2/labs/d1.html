Generate Ideas Across a Range

Use Copilot to generate options from conventional to unexpected — then combine two to find something neither produces alone.

8 – 10 min A real naming, framing, or options task M365 Copilot Premium
1
Describe your task
You
▶
Do this
Pick a real naming, framing, or options task from your current work — a new process, a project name, a way to position something. Write one sentence describing it before opening Copilot.
2
Generate with range
Copilot Chat
▶
Do this
Open Copilot Chat. Paste the prompt below. Replace the brackets.
Prompt 1 — Generate with range

Generate 10 options for [describe the task]. Range from most conventional to most unexpected. For each option: a one-sentence explanation of why it works. Include the unexpected ones even if they seem wrong — do not filter.

◉
Look for
Does the conventional end match what you would have produced yourself? Does the unexpected end include anything genuinely surprising? If neither end surprises you, the prompt may be too constrained — add the instruction: "push further toward the unexpected."
3
Synthesise two options
Copilot Chat
▶
Do this
Pick two options that feel most interesting but different from each other. Run the synthesis prompt:
Prompt 2 — Synthesise

What would a version look like that combines option [A] and option [B]? What does the combination have that neither option alone captures?

4
Make it yours
You
▶
Do this
Take your preferred output. Make one change that only you could make — a reference to your team, your client, or your specific situation. That edit is what makes it yours rather than a generic AI output.
Key Takeaways
  • Without explicit prompting toward the unconventional, Copilot defaults to the statistically common. "Range from most conventional to most unexpected" is the instruction that changes this.
  • The explanation Copilot writes for each option is often more useful than the option itself — it reveals what you are actually looking for.
  • Volume is not quality. Ask for the synthesis after generating — the combination is usually better than any single output from the first pass.
